#+title: What the probability means
#+language: en

* What the probability means

How the engine makes a probability, how well it held, and where it should not
be trusted.

A similarity between two faces is not a probability. The engine turns it into
one with a curve fitted on Labeled Faces in the Wild (LFW): 6000 pairs of
photos known to be of one person or of two. The curve was checked on folds of
pairs it had never seen. The fitting and its data are in the engine's project
(=calibrate.py= and =calibration.json=).

** How it held on LFW

These are scored on folds the curve never saw.

| Measure                            | Result                       |
|------------------------------------+------------------------------|
| Pairs right at 50%                 | 98.8% (+-0.3% between folds) |
| Brier score                        | 0.010                        |
| Pairs said 0-5% likely: one person | 0.9%                         |
| Said 5-20%: one person             | 8.7%                         |
| Said 20-45%: one person            | 21.6%                        |
| Said 80-95%: one person            | 94.7%                        |
| Said 95% and over: one person      | 100%                         |

AuraFace's makers report 99.65% on LFW with their own face detector. This
engine finds faces with YuNet, whose license is settled, and measures 98.8%.

Probabilities are shown from 1% to 99%, the ends of the ODNI's scale. No
calibration on 6000 pairs supports more certainty than that.

In a photo of several people, a master's face is the largest one. A
candidate's face is whichever of its faces is likeliest.

** Where it should not be trusted

- *Even prior odds.* LFW's pairs are half the same person, so the probability
  says how far the faces move the odds from 50%. If you have reason to think a
  match is rare, such as one suspect among thousands of photos, the real
  probability is lower than shown.
- *News photos.* LFW is mostly frontal, well-lit news photography. Profile
  views, poor light, low resolution and age gaps lower accuracy, and the
  calibration says nothing about how much.
- *Several masters.* The curve was fitted on one photo against one. A template
  of several masters raises genuine scores by design, so the probabilities for a
  template are somewhat generous.
- *Ethnicity.* AuraFace's makers say its training data may not cover all
  ethnicities well.

Treat the ranking as a way to find which photos to examine, not as an
identification.
